XEPL · XLALIGA · XWORLDCUPTWIT · live-pipeline — how the win-prob model was trained

One model,
four sites

The in-play win-probability model behind the live pipeline and all three dashboards was trained on every match I have ever scraped: 3,272 matches and 82,310 shots — four seasons of the EPL and La Liga, plus the World Cups of 2018, 2022 and 2026. This page tells the story of that fit: the data, the method, and the honest back-test.

the whole idea in 20 seconds
  • Before kick-off, the model expects a goals rate for each team — learned from all 82,310 shots, weighted toward recent form.
  • During the match, the score, the clock and recent xG turn that rate into P(home) / P(draw) / P(away), minute by minute.
  • One fit ships everywhere: the pipeline demo, this site, and the EPL, La Liga and World Cup dashboards all read the same numbers.

01 the data

Five corpora, one schema

Each of my scrapers writes one small JS file per match, and they all share one schema. That accident of consistency is what makes a single model possible: the corpus loader can read a 2022 Burnley match and a 2026 World Cup semi-final with the same code. Every one of the 82,310 shots carries the two things this model needs — a minute and an xG value (from my own xG model, explained in this page's sibling).

03 the method

Six decisions, step by step

The fit is a Dixon-Coles-lite: nothing exotic, every piece checkable. Each decision below exists because the data forced it.

01
Each competition gets its own baseline μ.

An EPL goal habitat is not a World Cup goal habitat — different defending, different stakes, different scoring rates. So the model never compares a team to "football in general", only to its own competition's baseline: the weighted mean single-team 90-minute xG. Fitted: EPL 1.42, La Liga 1.29, WC 1.34.

02
Team rates are shrunk toward that baseline.

A club seen for 152 matches earns its rate. A World Cup side seen three times does not — so every team's attack rate is pulled toward μ by k pseudo-matches of average evidence (empirical-Bayes shrinkage). k was tuned over {1, 2, 4, 8, 16} by holdout Brier: k = 1 won. Teams are namespaced by competition — EPL/Arsenal and WC/Argentina never mix.

att_T = (Σ w·xG_for + k·μ) / (Σ w + k)
03
Old seasons fade at 0.8 per season.

Every match is weighted 0.8seasons ago, so 2025-26 form dominates a club's rate and 2022-23 still whispers. World Cup editions age in 4-year steps — WC 2022 is one season behind WC 2026, not four.

04
Home advantage is measured, not assumed.

Fitted as sqrt(mean home xG / mean away xG) over league matches only: ×1.13 to the home side's rate. World Cup venues are neutral, so the boost is switched off there entirely.

05
The clock is the market's clock, not the referee's.

The model prices the 1X2 (90 min) market. Stoppage time counts: a 90+4′ winner settles the market, so those events fold into minute 90. World Cup knockout extra time never enters — a tie level at 90′ settles as a draw, whatever happens after.

06
Pre-match rates hand off to the live model.

The fitted rates set each team's pre-match expectation, then the live formula blends it with in-match momentum (xG pace over the last 15 minutes, weight w = 0.35) and scales by the minutes left. The full in-play treatment — Poisson grid, odds, settlement — is taught in the live pipeline demo, so it isn't re-taught here.

lam = att·opp_def/μ (±hfa)  →  lam_rem(t) = ((90−t)/90)·[(1−w)·λ_pre + w·90·pace₁₅]

05 the honest benchmark

Tested blind on 864 matches it never saw

The design in one sentence: train on 2,408 matches (league seasons 2022-23 to 2024-25 plus World Cups 2018 and 2022), then test blind on 864 (the 2025-26 league seasons plus World Cup 2026). Brier score against the 90-minute result, checked at seven points in the match — lower is better.

Both model variants beat always quoting the base rates, at every checkpoint. And the honest part: the no-momentum variant (w = 0) edges out the momentum blend at this scale. Fifteen minutes of xG is a noisy signal — a team can look dominant for a quarter of an hour and mean nothing by full time — and w = 0.35 overweights it. The production parameters keep w = 0.35 so momentum stays visible in the live demo, but the table reports both so the number is never oversold.
